# Decisions of step 0d (answers and guardrails)

Started 2026-11-03, after the review gate of 0c (`0004`). One decision at a time, with options; this file lists what is decided and what is not.

| # | Decision | Who, when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | An answer cites the article **and the exact passage** (not the article alone) | User, 2026-11-03 |
| 2 | Granularity G1: the retrieved chunk is the source shown to the model; inside it the text is split into numbered **legal units** (sentence, list item, GDPR paragraph or point). The model answers with the ids of the units it used; the passage shown to the reader is the stored text of those units, never text written by the model. A list item is shown with its lead-in. Rejected: chunk-only citation (not the exact passage), fixed windows inside the chunk | User, 2026-11-03 |
| 3 | No provider citation feature (Anthropic Citations API): it ties the project to one provider and a higher price; the LLM stays behind one adapter | User, 2026-11-03 |
| 4 | L2: LlamaIndex as the orchestration layer around our own retrieval (a retriever that calls our exact search, a node post-processor that numbers the units, a query engine that calls the DeepSeek connector's `chat` in JSON mode: `structured_predict` hides the token counts that the trace needs). The id resolution, the refusal rule and the tracing stay in plain code, tested without the framework. Rejected: the full LlamaIndex stack (not the retrieval we measured), our code only with LlamaIndex later | User, 2026-11-03 |
| 5 | In the answer metrics, our design is compared with LlamaIndex's own `CitationQueryEngine` (same retrieval, same model, same dev questions): answer support, citation precision, passage length, refusals, cost and speed. If the default is as good and simpler, we consider using it | User, 2026-11-03 |
| 6 | Legal-unit rule (`src/legalrag/units.py`): a unit is a slice of the chunk text (sentence; numbered list item 1°, 2°… incl. "bis"; GDPR point a), b)); a numbered list counts only if it opens a line or follows a colon, so "aux 1° et 2° de l'article…" is not a list; a GDPR paragraph number ("1.") stays with its sentence; an item or point is shown with its lead-in, with " […] " where units are skipped | Assistant, to confirm, 2026-11-03 |
| 7 | The answer prompt holds the **top 5 articles** (their best-matching chunk, numbered into legal units). Measured on the 58 answerable dev questions: any gold article in the top 5 for 90%, all primary gold for 79% (top 8: 93% and 86%; top 10: 93% and 88%); median prompt 4,000 characters. Rejected: 8 articles (recommended by the assistant), an adaptive count | User, 2026-11-03 |

| 8 | Tracing and failure tracking (S1): a Postgres table `query_traces`, one row per request (question, outcome, top 5 articles with scores, cited units, answer, tokens, cost, latency, model, prompt, code and data versions, hashed client id, optional feedback, expiry after 30 days), plus JSON logs to stdout with a request id and no question text. Outcomes: answered, refused_weak_retrieval, refused_by_model, invalid_citation, parse_error, llm_error, rate_limited, llm_disabled. Commands to list failures, export candidates for the eval set (reviewed by the user first) and purge expired rows. The development ledger (`data/llm_ledger.jsonl`) stays a file. Rejected: JSONL files only (the service is stateless), an external tracing service now (kept as a possible exporter) | User, 2026-11-03 |

| 9 | Refusal rule R3, two stages plus code checks: (1) the answer is not attempted when the top-1 similarity is below a threshold, provisionally 0.60 (on the 65 dev questions it refuses 1 of 52 answerable questions that have their evidence in the top 5, and 6 of the 13 others; tuned on few questions, specific to bge-m3 and the current chunking, to be re-checked from real failures); (2) the model may refuse; (3) code turns any answer without a valid cited unit into a refusal. The reason is stored in `refusal_reason` of the trace. Rejected: score threshold only, model only, an LLM relevance grader before every answer | User, 2026-11-03 |

| 10 | The answer model is **`deepseek-flash`, thinking off**, temperature 0. Pilot on the 65 dev questions, prompt `answer.v1`: flash and `deepseek-v4-pro` give the same outcomes (53 of 58 answerable answered, 51 citing a gold article, 5 of 7 other questions refused, no dropped citation); v4-pro costs 4 times more (0.094 against 0.024 USD) and is twice as slow (3.4 against 1.7 s median). Without the pre-filter flash answers 54 of 58 and refuses the same 5 of 7 others. Counts say nothing about truth; that needs the validated judge. Rejected: v4-pro, waiting for the judge. Threshold 0.60 not yet re-tuned | User, 2026-11-08 |

| 11 | The pre-filter threshold is **0.55** (was 0.60 in decision 9). Replay of the 65 dev questions with flash: 0.60 stopped 3 answerable questions (2 of which the model answers) and 4 of 6 unanswerable or false-premise ones, all of which the model refuses anyway; 0.55 stops no answerable question and 1 of the 6; the model alone refused all 4 unanswerable questions. So the filter is a cost and safety guard, not a measured gain. Rejected: 0.60, removing the filter | User, 2026-11-08 |

| 12 | The working answer prompt is **`answer.v3`** (v2 plus: every factual word must appear in the cited units; a negation never becomes a condition; no examples the units do not name). 65 dev questions, flash, threshold 0.55: v1 123 words per answer, 41% of claims more than 80% copied from the cited text; v2 76 words, 2%; v3 72 words, 5%. v1 to v3 outcomes: 55 of 58 answerable answered, 5 of 7 others refused, 0 dropped citations; cites a gold article in 51 (v2 52, v1 52). v2 wrote "ancienneté" as a condition of leave, which L3164-9 says does not matter; v3 states it correctly. Faithfulness is not measured yet: it needs the validated judge, and the labelled set must include v3 answers. Rejected: v1 (long, copies the source), v2 (one measured drift) | User, 2026-11-08 |
| 13 | Rate limits count in Postgres, from the `query_traces` table (requests per hashed client and per day, cost per day), before each request; the kill switch is a flag row in the database, read on each request and changed by a command without a restart. When the flag is off or a limit is reached, search still works and the outcome is `llm_disabled` or `rate_limited`. Rejected: memory of one process (resets on restart, not shared), Redis (a further service to run; to reconsider with several servers), an environment variable for the switch (needs a restart) | User, 2026-11-09 |
| 14 | Limit values (demo-sized, kept in settings): 5 questions per client per minute, 30 per client per day, 0.10 USD of model cost per day for the whole service (the user lowered it from the proposed 1 USD). Measured cost per answered question with `answer.v3` and `deepseek-flash`: mean 0.00037 USD, maximum 0.00075 (64 calls), so 0.10 USD is about 270 questions. Only requests that reach the model count toward the limits; requests refused by a limit or by the weak-retrieval check do not. The client id is a daily-salted hash, so a client is counted within one day only and people behind one address count as one client. Rejected: tight (3 per minute, 15 per day, 0.25 USD), loose (10 per minute, 100 per day, 5 USD) | User, 2026-11-09 |
| 15 | When a limit is reached or the model is off, the visitor still gets the top 5 articles (label, section title, link) and one line saying why there is no written answer (client limit with the wait time, daily cost cap, answers switched off, weak retrieval). The HTTP status carries the reason for machines: 429 for a client limit, 503 for the cost cap or the kill switch. Search costs nothing, so it is never limited by the cost cap. Rejected: an error with no articles (looks like a broken demo), a queue (wrong for an interactive demo) | User, 2026-11-09 |
| 16 | The claim judge is **`deepseek-flash`, no thinking, prompt `judge.v2`** (`prompts/judge.yaml`, `claimjudge.py`, `scripts/judge_claims.py`), validated against 49 hand-labelled claims (`data/labels/answers.labels.json`: 26 natural, 19 edited with one controlled change each, 4 unchanged; 11 labels corrected after the labeller re-read the texts): agreement 82%, kappa 0.68 (95% interval about 0.49 to 0.84), 17 of 17 errors caught, 8 of 32 good claims flagged. Other runs: v4-pro v2 kappa 0.57, flash and v4-pro with thinking 0.51 (thinking makes the judge stricter), v1 prompt 0.44 to 0.48, a Claude model run from the session 0.76 (not callable from the code). The differences between the non-thinking judges are within the noise of 49 claims. The v2 prompt was written after seeing the disagreements, so the figures are optimistic; flash is also the answer model (self-preference not measurable on this set). The judge's share of supported claims is read as a lower bound. To redo when the answer model or prompt changes. Rejected: v4-pro (no gain, about 4 times the cost), thinking mode, an Anthropic key now | User, 2026-11-09 |
| 17 | The answer metrics are faithfulness (the judge of decision 16 on every claim; shares of supported, partly and not supported claims, and of answers with all claims supported, read as lower bounds), citations (answers citing a gold or primary gold article; share of cited articles that are gold, which understates precision because the gold lists are not complete) and refusals (answerable questions refused, split into retrieval misses and refusals despite evidence in the top 5; unanswerable and false-premise questions refused), plus cost and time. Completeness is not measured: the user judged it too hard to evaluate, in particular for ambiguous questions, and the aim is to retrieve the relevant articles, answer as far as the sources allow and give the references. Code: `answermetrics.py`, `scripts/answer_metrics.py` | User, 2026-11-10 |

| 18 | Our answer path is kept; LlamaIndex's `CitationQueryEngine` is not adopted (decision 5 comparison, same retrieval, model, questions and judge): faithfulness and gold citations are the same (supported claims 85% against 86%, not supported 2.4% against 2.4%, answers citing a gold article 93% against 90%); with its default 512-token chunks the engine shows the reader 920 characters on average against 311 for our unit (decision 6: the exact passage was the aim; the engine accepts a custom `text_splitter`, so a unit splitter could close this gap, not tested, and it would not carry our lead-in), writes uncited sentences, and gives no refusal signal (5 of 6 unanswerable or false-premise questions refused by our path, 1 of 6 structurally by the engine, 5 more in words with citations attached). The engine is simpler (no splitter, resolver or refusal code) and uses 12% more input tokens. Rejected: adopting the engine; testing it with a unit splitter | User, 2026-11-10 |

| 19 | English GDPR check with ClaimRAG-LAW (CC BY 4.0, data kept out of git; paper arXiv 2605.21071): our English corpus has the same text but one article per unit while their chunks span 2 or 3 articles (gold for retrieval = the articles that overlap the chunk, rule to state before use); the 149 questions use 39 chunks, about 15 articles. Completeness is measured only there, on the reference claims the expert labelled Entailment (292 of 520), with a looser judge prompt `judge.v3` (held-out test half: agreement 72%, kappa 0.44, against 68% and 0.39 for `judge.v2`; faithfulness keeps `judge.v2`), and is indicative: no expert label covers the task "does our answer say this claim". Triples are turned into sentences by a plain template (a model rewrite copied the template in 310 of 450 cases and scored the same). The outside labels do not validate `judge.v2`: its kappa on them is 0.32 (0.68 on our own labels) because the expert's Entailment is looser than "the passage states it"; the labels are relative to the claim file's own short chunk (with the longer chunk of the RAG file the judge agrees worse: kappa 0.29 against 0.42 on the 398 claims whose question is in both files, matched by question text since the ids drift) | User, 2026-11-10 |


Measured on the 12,136 default chunks (2026-11-03): no violation of the slice rules (every unit is a slice, in order, only whitespace between units); median 2 units per chunk, 99% at most 16, largest 81; unit length median 165 characters, 90% at most 364, largest 3,745; 2,282 lists or point groups, 2,280 introduced by a colon (the 2 others are GDPR definitions). The old sentence splitter treated 43% of the numbered lists as one sentence. The abbreviation "art." followed by a capital or a digit does not occur in the corpus, so the known limit of the sentence splitter on "l'art." has no effect.

Facts behind the decisions (measured 2026-11-03): the median chunk has 2 sentences and 229 tokens, the largest 42 sentences; 22% of the 11,425 Code du travail articles contain a numbered list (1°, 2°…), and 43% of those (1,068) are a single sentence for the old splitter; 82 of 99 French GDPR articles have numbered paragraphs and 44 have (a), (b) points. `llama-index-core` 0.14.25 with `llama-index-llms-deepseek` 0.4.1: 69 packages, 148 MB, 5.5 s import, 141 MB of memory; the connector accepts `deepseek-flash` and `structured_predict` returned a validated answer (it works through prompt parsing, the connector reports no function calling).
