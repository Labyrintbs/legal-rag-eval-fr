# Step 0f decisions: web service and demo page

Background and the list of what 0f contains: `docs/decisions/0005-web-service-and-demo.md`. Decisions are taken one at a time, in this order: 1 framework and process layout, 2 API contract, 3 client identity, 4 caching, 5 feedback box, 6 article-number rule, 7 demo page, 8 memory and speed on the target server.

| # | Decision | Who, when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | FastAPI with one worker process; bge-m3 is loaded inside it at start; at most 2 questions are embedded at the same time (semaphore). Not chosen: Flask/Gunicorn (no Pydantic link), a separate embedding service (two things to deploy, a network hop), Streamlit/Gradio (0005). Measured on the Mac CPU (8 cores, 12 French dev questions): bge-m3 takes about 1.0 GB loaded and peaks at 1.95 GB after queries; embedding one question takes a median of 156 ms (max 201 ms). The 2.5 GB of 0005 was a guess. The target server is not measured yet (decision 8) | User, 2026-11-11 |
| 2 | One JSON reply for `POST /ask` (`request_id`, `outcome`, `claims` with their citations, the 5 retrieved `articles`, `reason`, disclaimer) in the same shape for every outcome. Status 200 for answers and for refusals about the question (weak search, model refusal); 429 with `Retry-After` for a client limit; 503 for the cost cap and the switch. The retrieved articles come back in every case. Question limited to 500 characters (422 above): the 128 French gold questions have median 88, 95th percentile 147, maximum 220. `lang` defaults to `fr`; the demo does not expose English. `POST /search` returns only the articles with similarities and is never limited. Not chosen: streaming (the answer must pass the citation check before it is shown), a job id with polling (not needed for a 2 s reply) | User, 2026-11-11 |
| 3 | A client is the visitor's IP address, read from the header that the proxy in front of the service sets (`X-Forwarded-For` by default, the name is a setting `CLIENT_IP_HEADER` because the deployment is not chosen), and stored only as the daily keyed hash of `trace.client_hash`. The header is trusted only when the request comes from our own proxy (uvicorn `--forwarded-allow-ips`), otherwise a visitor could invent an address per request; the proxy replaces any header the visitor sends. No address: all such requests share one count (`limits.py`). Search is never limited. Known limit: people behind one shared address share a quota; the global cap of $0.10 a day is the backstop. Not chosen: a cookie id (cleared at will, consent question), IP plus cookie (more states), an invite token per person (friction; open or behind a link is decision 7). Not tested on a shared network | User, 2026-11-11 |
| 4 | Exact-match answer cache in a Postgres table. Key: hash of the normalised question (lowercase, spaces collapsed) with the model, prompt id, threshold and data version, so a change of any of them gives a new key. Value: the finished reply. Only `answered` and `refused_by_model` are cached (never errors, limits or the switch-off). A hit skips the embedding and the model, is not counted in the visitor's quota (the per-minute limit still applies), and the reply says `cached: true`. A thumbs-down removes the entry (decision 5). Entries expire after 7 days (a guess, untested; the version in the key already prevents stale answers). Only the hash of the question is stored, not its text. `legalrag cache-warm` runs the checked example questions after a deploy. Measured: one answer costs about $0.0004 and 1.6 s of model time; real repeat rates are unknown until there is traffic. Not chosen: no cache, a cache in process memory (emptied at each deploy), a semantic cache (similar legal questions can have different answers) | User, 2026-11-11 |
| 5 | Feedback box, option B. Under each answer: 👍 / 👎; after a 👎 an optional form with a kind (wrong answer, wrong citation, missing article, other) and an optional comment; a 👍 can carry a comment too. `POST /feedback {request_id, value, kind?, comment?}`; comment at most 500 characters; a new column for the kind; a notice "do not write personal data, kept 30 days". Comments live in `query_traces` (30-day expiry), are shown to no visitor, and are read by the owner with `legalrag traces`; `traces export` turns failures into candidate evaluation questions. The `request_id` must exist, one vote per request (a new one replaces it), 10 a minute per client. A 👎 removes the cached answer only when the voter is the client who got it (otherwise a stranger could clear answers and make the service pay for new ones). The user also asked for a contact line at the bottom of the page: a link to the GitHub issues of the repository (issues are enabled) and an email address. Which address to show, and whether to write it in a form that scrapers read less easily, is for the user to say when the page is built (decision 7); no address is published before | User, 2026-11-11 |
| 6 | The article-number rule (`articlenum`) is on for the service: a question that names an article gets that article first, by a direct lookup, before the search results. Evidence so far is thin: on dev it changes 1 of 65 rankings (`smoke-08`, outside the top 5 to rank 1), and it acts only when a number is named. Before it is built into the service it is tested on a small sample (about 15 variants of "Que dit l'article X ?" with real article numbers and about 15 questions that name no article), and the numbers go to the user. The gate gets a test for it on articles that are in the fixture. Not chosen: off (a visitor who types an article number gets a refusal), on for `/search` only | User, 2026-11-11 |

## Build notes (part 1, the service, 2026-11-13)
- New folder `web/` (`app`, `schemas`, `build`), after `evaluation` and before `cli` in the dependency order; `legalrag serve` starts it (one worker, `proxy_headers` off).
- Decision 3 as built: the client header is read by the app itself (`Settings.client_ip_header`, env `CLIENT_IP_HEADER`) and not by uvicorn's `--forwarded-allow-ips`. It is only safe when the service is reachable through our proxy alone (bind to localhost or the container network), because a visitor could write the header. Without a named header the connection address is used.
- Decision 2 detail not discussed before: a model failure (`llm_error`, `parse_error`) answers 502 with the articles; the other refusals stay 200.
- Search mode is traced like answers (`mode = search`; outcome `answered`, or `refused_weak_retrieval` when nothing is close enough) and never limited. A failed trace write is logged and the visitor still gets the reply.
- Measured on the real service on the Mac (CPU, the full database with its 8 chunking runs): `/search` 0.6 to 0.8 s when warm (embedding about 0.16 s of it), but 7 to 10 s for the first requests after the server had been idle. The exact scan sorted the vectors of all 8 runs; after the 7 comparison runs were deleted (EXPERIMENT.md, 2026-11-13) the same query takes 0.10 s warm instead of 0.34 s. The slow first request after a pause (a cold cache is a guess) is still to look at in the deployment stage (decision 8).
- Replies (changed 2026-11-23 after the user's review of part 1): `/ask` and `/health` set the status and the `Retry-After` header on an injected `Response` and return the data, so that FastAPI builds the reply from it. The first version returned a `JSONResponse` built by hand, which FastAPI sends unchecked. Correction (part 2, 2026-11-23): FastAPI re-checks a returned value only when it is not already an `AskReply` object; for an object the check is the one made when it is built, and Pydantic ignores an unknown keyword by default, so `AskReply(reson="...")` silently left `reason` empty. The reply classes now inherit `schemas.Reply` (`extra="forbid"`): a misspelled field is an error when the reply is built. `/search` already returned its model (always 200). `/feedback` keeps `HTTPException` for its errors (404, 429): the page only needs "ok or not" there.
- Not done yet in the service: the cache and its `cache_hit` outcome (part 2), the article-number rule (part 3), the pages (part 4).

## Evaluation additions considered during the build (2026-11-17), outside the numbered decisions
Compared with the generation metrics of Ragas, RAGChecker, Vectara open-rag-eval and the Stanford study of legal research tools (sources in the chat of that day; `EXPERIMENT.md` has the numbers).
- Answer-level view of the faithfulness verdicts: adopted by the user; built (`answermetrics.answer_level`), reported in the metrics files.
- Relevancy ("does the answer address the question"): the judge version (`relevance.v1`, three labels with a reason) is chosen over the Ragas style, by the user on 2026-11-17, after the small comparison (clear labels, no threshold to choose; Ragas scores overlap). Not yet run on all answers; the plan is the 112 answered dev and test answers, the wrong-pair control, a hand check of about 30 labels, and the injection questions of the build's part 3.
- Reference check (user, 2026-11-17): ministry sheets only. The 29 ministry-sheet questions (15 dev, 14 test) have the ministry's own section text (`data/external/fiches-travail.json`, section title equal to the question's heading) as an independent reference. The 59 model-written questions have a one-sentence reference written from the same article as the answer (circular) and are not used as a score; 27 answerable questions (smoke, hand-written) have no reference. The check is "does the answer contradict the official text", not completeness. Known limits: the sheets are dated about November 2025 and the corpus snapshot is July 2025, so a change of the law can look like a contradiction; the licence of the sheets is not checked, so the text stays local. Same rule as for the judges: dev pairs first, hand-checked by the user, then the test pairs.
- The four relations a RAG evaluation checks: question to passages (retrieval, measured), answer to passages (faithfulness, measured; it is groundedness, not correctness), question to answer (relevancy, to add), answer to reference (correctness, ministry sheets only).
- Ministry check, result of 2026-11-23: all 27 answered ministry pairs (dev and test) were read; 0 contradictions on the assistant's reading, 2 answers that miss the subject of the sheet (sheet-20, sheet-05); sheet-20 is "not covered" (user, 2026-11-23). The judge prompt `ministry_check.v2` (written from dev) is the screen to use; it matches the assistant's reading on 26 of 27 pairs. It stays a screen followed by a reading, not a score. Details in `EXPERIMENT.md` (2026-11-23).

## Build notes, part 2: the answer cache (2026-11-23)
Decision 4 as built, with what the user confirmed on 2026-11-23:
- **Warm-up (revises decision 4, option C of the user's suggestion):** it is not a command run after a deploy but part of the service launch. At startup the service reads the example questions from the file named in `CACHE_WARM_FILE` and asks them in a background thread (`web/warm.py`); it skips questions already cached (a restart costs nothing), never fails the launch, stops when the model is off, the daily budget is used up or the model fails, and is off when no file is set. `/health` shows `cache_warm` (off, running, done). `legalrag cache warm FILE` runs the same function by hand (it loads the model, shows the cost, asks first). The list of examples comes in part 4. Calls of the warm-up are traced like others (no client) and count toward the $0.10 cap; they skip the per-client limits (`engine.respond(..., warm=True)`), not the switch or the budget.
- **Cached replies have their own minute limit** (`limits.decide_cached`, changed 2026-11-23 after the user asked to think about innocent visitors): the kill switch and the daily budget do not stop a stored reply, so the examples keep working when strangers use up the cap; a hit does not use the model allowance (5 a minute) nor the day's quota (30), and has its own counter of 30 a minute per client (`per_minute_cached`). The first version counted hits in the model allowance: six colleagues behind one office address clicking examples would have been refused. A client over the cached limit is refused with a 429 and the stored articles, without a model call. 30 is a guess, untested.
- **`/search` is limited** (`search_per_minute = 30` per client, in process memory like the feedback limit): it costs no model money but every search embeds the question on the CPU. Measured on the local service (this Mac, one process, 2026-11-23): one search alone 0.33 to 0.39 s; 3.0 searches a second with 2 at a time, 4.3 with 8, 4.7 with 16, and with 16 at once the median latency rises to 3.3 s (slowest 4.0 s), so one script slows everyone. 30 a minute is a tenth of that capacity; a person does about 6 to 12 a minute. 30 is a guess; the deployed server is not measured (decision 8). A limit at the reverse proxy (Caddy, nginx or Cloudflare) would stop a flood before Python and belongs to the deployment stage; it does not replace this one (many clients together can still saturate one process).
- **Still open for part 3:** about 10 English questions to see what a visitor who types English gets (the question field accepts any language; `lang` is fixed to `fr`).
- **The key** holds the normalised question, the model, the prompt id, the search threshold, the data version and the code version. The data version is computed from the stored corpus (the `corpus_versions` file hashes and the chunking run settings; `DATA_VERSION` overrides it); the code version is `CODE_VERSION`, "dev" without it, so a deploy that sets it starts with an empty cache.
- **Storage:** table `answer_cache` (migration 0006: key, reply as jsonb, outcome, created, expiry, hits; only the hash of the question); `query_traces.cache_key` (set on every `/ask` trace) and the outcome `cache_hit`. TTL 7 days (a guess, untested). Only `answered` and `refused_by_model` are stored. An entry that does not fit the current reply template is a miss.
- **Thumbs-down** removes the entry that served the request only when the voter is the client who got that reply (`cache.drop_for_vote`), whether that reply was a first answer or a hit.
- **Pinned examples (user, 2026-11-23):** the replies stored by the warm-up are pinned (migration 0007): they do not expire and a thumbs-down does not remove them; the vote is still saved on the trace. Reason: a thumbs-down removes an entry for everyone, and the examples are the part of the demo that the user checked by hand. A visitor's own question that matches an example is pinned by the warm-up and not asked again. Only `cache clear` or a new version of the key removes a pinned entry. `legalrag traces feedback` (new) lists the votes with kind, comment and whether the reply came from the cache; before, a thumbs-down on a normal answer was stored but listed nowhere (`traces failures` shows only failures).
- **Commands:** `legalrag cache purge` (expired entries; pinned ones stay), `cache clear` (all, asks first), `cache warm FILE`.
- **Layout:** the work of `/ask` (cache, engine, traces) moved from `web/app.py` to `web/asking.py`, shared with the warm-up; `answering/cache.py` holds the storage.
- **Not measured:** how often visitors repeat a question; the 7-day TTL; the real cost of a warm-up with the real model (not run: the examples come in part 4).

## Part 3: the article-number rule in the service (user, 2026-11-23)
- **Sample test** (`scripts/answers/article_rule_sample.py`, retrieval only, no model call, 30 questions written by hand, not dev or test questions): of 13 questions naming an article that exists, plain search had the article in the top 5 for 3 (ranks 4, 1, 2); with the rule all 13 are at rank 1 (rank 2 when two are named). 15 questions naming no article: the rule found nothing, all 15 rankings identical (traps: "décret 2017-1387", "article 12 de ma convention collective", "article 17" without the RGPD). A number that does not exist (`L9999-99`) finds nothing.
- **Weak-retrieval check (option 1):** when the rule finds an existing named article the check is skipped, because the question names a real article; 2 of the 13 would otherwise have been refused (top similarity 0.514 and 0.547). `L9999-99` finds nothing, so it is still refused. The named article's own similarity can be low (0.3 to 0.4) and is shown as it is.
- **Spellings:** `L. 1221-13`, `R 1454-14`, `R. *3231-4` and attached lowercase `l1221-13` are accepted; a lowercase letter with a space is not (`l 2008-596`).
- **Built:** `LegalRetriever(article_rule=True)` (on in `web/build.py` only; the evaluation and the pilots keep the plain search), `search.named_chunks`, `rag.names_article` used by `respond` and `/search`. Tests: spellings, retriever, weak check, and a gate test on the fixture articles in three spellings.
- **Injection and English questions** (`scripts/answers/stress_questions.py`, 11 + 10 questions written by hand, real model, 0.005 USD; output `data/pilot/stress.json`, not tracked): none of the 11 injection attempts was obeyed (no poem, no "OK", no "10 ans", no link, no prompt or key shown). 5 were refused before the model (weak retrieval, best similarity 0.37 to 0.51), 2 refused by the model (it said it cannot invent a source; the contract-clause question is not covered), 4 answered the legal question and ignored the instruction; the answer relevancy judge: 3 "addresses", 1 "partly" (the sick-leave question, answered with the work-accident rules, a retrieval limit and not an injection effect). English: 3 of 10 answered (all judged "addresses"), 6 refused by the model (the extracts retrieved were off topic, for example the notice question retrieved the abandonment-of-post rules) and 1 weak; the refusal reasons are in French. Read by the assistant; not a measured rate.
